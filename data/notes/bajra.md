# Bajra / pearl millet (बाजरी) — kharif notes for Maharashtra

Teaching data. Typical ranges from public extension advice; confirm with your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra before acting.

## Where and when
- Suited to light and medium soils in the rain shadow: Ahmednagar, Nashik, Pune, Satara, Solapur, Dhule, Jalgaon. It survives short dry spells that would kill soybean.
- Sow from the last week of June to the third week of July after 50 mm of rain. Sowing in August gives a poor crop because grain filling runs into the dry period.

## Seed and seed rate
- 3–4 kg per hectare of hybrid or improved seed (Dhanshakti, Phule Adishakti, ICTP-8203, or a private hybrid). Bajra seed is tiny, so mix it with sand or fine soil for even sowing.
- Treat seed with Metalaxyl (6 g per kg) against downy mildew and Thiram (2 g per kg). Where ergot is a problem, dip seed in 10 percent salt water and discard floating seed.

## Spacing and depth
- Rows 45 cm apart, plants 15 cm apart. Depth 2–3 cm; deeper sowing is the commonest reason for a thin stand.
- Thin to one plant per hill 10–15 days after emergence. Fill gaps by transplanting thinned seedlings on a cloudy day.

## Nutrients
- Per hectare: 60 kg N, 30 kg P2O5 on medium soil; halve it on shallow soil. Give half the N at sowing and half at 30 days if rain allows.
- Zinc sulphate 20 kg per hectare once in three years on sandy soils.

## Weeds
- One weeding and hoeing at 20 days. Bajra closes its canopy fast, so a second weeding is rarely needed.

## Pests and diseases
- Downy mildew (green ear): the ear turns into leafy green shoots. Seed treatment plus resistant varieties prevents it; uproot infected plants.
- Ergot: sticky honeydew on ears in wet weather; harvest ergot-affected ears separately and never feed them to animals.
- Shoot fly on late sowings: sow on time and increase seed rate slightly if sowing late.

## Harvest
- Ready 80–90 days after sowing when the grains are hard and cannot be dented by a thumbnail. Cut ears first, then the stalks for fodder.
- Expected yield: 15–20 quintals of grain per hectare with hybrids, plus 40–50 quintals of dry fodder.
