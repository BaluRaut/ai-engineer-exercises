# Kharif calendar and choosing a crop — Maharashtra

Teaching data. Typical ranges from public extension advice; confirm with your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra before acting.

## The season in one line
- Kharif is the monsoon season. Sowing runs mid June to mid July, the crop grows through the rains, and harvest runs from late September (bajra, early soybean) to January (late tur, cotton).

## Month by month
- May: repair bunds, buy seed from a licensed dealer and keep the bill, get a soil test, sow the kharif onion nursery.
- June: wait for 75–100 mm of rain before sowing soybean, cotton and tur. Sow bajra in the last week if rain has come. Treat all seed.
- July: finish sowing by 15 July at the latest. Transplant kharif onion. Gap fill within 10 days of emergence. First weeding at 20 days.
- August: second weeding, top dressing for cotton and onion, pest scouting twice a week. Watch drainage after heavy rain.
- September: soybean pod filling, cotton flowering, pink bollworm traps in place. Do not spray at random; count pests first.
- October: harvest soybean and bajra, harvest kharif onion, first cotton picking. Dry grain before storage.
- November to January: cotton pickings, tur harvest, sow rabi crops on residual moisture.

## Choosing by soil and water
- Deep black soil, rain-fed: cotton, soybean plus tur intercrop, or sole tur. Avoid onion unless drainage is excellent.
- Medium soil, rain-fed: soybean plus tur, or bajra where rain is uncertain.
- Shallow or light soil: bajra, or a short-duration tur. Cotton on shallow soil fails in a dry year.
- Any soil with an assured well or drip: kharif onion, irrigated cotton, or vegetables.
- If sowing slips past 15 July: switch from soybean to bajra or a short-duration tur; do not sow cotton late.

## Seed rate summary per hectare
- Soybean 65–75 kg. Cotton about 2.5 packets of 450 g. Onion 8–10 kg for the transplanted field. Tur 12–15 kg sole, 4–5 kg as intercrop. Bajra 3–4 kg.

## Before spending money
- Get a soil test every three years; it usually saves more fertiliser than it costs.
- Buy seed and inputs only with a bill; it is your proof if the seed fails.
- Check the district agriculture office and your nearest KVK for this year's advisories, especially on new pests and on which varieties are being recommended.
